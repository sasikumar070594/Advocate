Civil Cases

General information on civil suits - how a civil case generally starts, what stages it has, and what documents are usually needed. Not advice on a specific case.

General information only: A civil case generally starts with a plaint stating facts, relief sought and court fee. The other side files a written statement. Then issues are framed, evidence is recorded, arguments are heard, and judgment is given. Common documents: ID proof, agreement / papers relating to the dispute, notices exchanged, and prior orders if any. Time taken depends on court, documents and the other side's response. This is not advice on a specific matter. Reference: Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 - general procedure.

High Court Appeals

General information on appeals before the High Court - what an appeal means and what is usually needed.

General information only: An appeal means asking a higher court to re-examine a lower court / tribunal order. Generally needed: certified copy of the judgment / order, the original case papers, grounds of appeal, and documents relied upon. Appeals have time limits - the limit depends on the type of order and the law under which it was passed. Missing the limit can affect the appeal. Consult with the full order copy without delay. This is not advice on a specific appeal. Reference: Code of Civil Procedure / Code of Criminal Procedure - general appeal procedure.

High Court Writs

General information on writ petitions under Article 226 - the 5 writs and when they are generally used.

General information only: Under Article 226 of the Constitution, a High Court can issue writs for enforcement of fundamental rights and for any other purpose. The 5 writs are: Habeas Corpus (to produce a person said to be illegally detained), Mandamus (to direct a public authority to perform a duty it is bound to do), Prohibition (to stop a lower court / tribunal from acting beyond jurisdiction), Certiorari (to quash an order passed without jurisdiction or in breach of natural justice), and Quo Warranto (to question a person's authority to hold a public office). Which writ applies depends on facts. This is not advice on a specific matter. Reference: Constitution of India, Article 226.

Cheque Cases

General information on cheque bounce cases under Section 138 NI Act - the 30 days / 15 days / 1 month timeline.

General information only: Under Section 138, Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, a cheque bounce becomes an offence only if: the cheque was for a legally recoverable debt, it was presented within its validity (cheques are generally valid for 3 months), a written demand notice was sent within 30 days of receiving the bank return memo, the drawer did not pay within 15 days of receiving the notice, and the complaint was filed within 1 month after that. Keep: cheque copy, return memo, notice copy with proof of delivery, and reply if any. This is not advice on a specific cheque. Reference: NI Act, Section 138 - procedure and time limits.

DVC Cases

General information on Domestic Violence Act cases - Section 12, reliefs, and procedure.

General information only: Under the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005, an aggrieved woman can approach a Protection Officer, service provider, police, or the Magistrate directly. A Domestic Incident Report (DIR) may be prepared. An application under Section 12 is filed before the Judicial Magistrate where the woman lives, where the respondent lives, or where the violence occurred. The Magistrate can grant: Protection Order (Sec 18), Residence Order (Sec 19), Monetary Relief including maintenance (Sec 20), Temporary Custody (Sec 21), and Compensation (Sec 22). Interim / ex parte orders can be passed under Section 23 where needed. Keep ID proof, DIR / complaint copy, and any medical / other papers safe. This is not advice on a specific case. Reference: PWDVA, 2005 - Sections 12, 18-23.

General information on real estate matters - agreements, approvals, and what to check before buying.

Accident Claim Cases

General information on Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT) claims - 6 months limit and procedure.

General information only: Compensation for death or injury in a road accident is claimed before the Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT) under Section 166, Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, against driver, owner and insurer. Since the 2019 amendment, the claim must generally be filed within 6 months of the accident. The claim can be filed where the accident happened, where the claimant lives, or where the defendant lives. Keep: FIR, medical records and bills, discharge summary, income proof, death certificate if applicable, and insurance details. This is not advice on a specific accident. Reference: Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, Sections 165-166.

Criminal Cases

General information on criminal cases - FIR, bail, trial stages, and what to do if you get a notice.

General information only: A criminal case generally starts with an FIR / complaint, investigation, and charge sheet. If you receive a notice / summons, do not ignore it - note the date, keep the notice safe, and consult with the notice and related papers. Bail is a request to the court to release a person pending trial, subject to conditions - it depends on facts, offence, and documents. Trial stages generally include charges, evidence, and arguments. Every case depends on its own facts. This is not advice on a specific criminal case. Reference: Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) / Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) - general procedure.

Society & Trust Registration

General information on Society and Trust registration in Tamil Nadu - what is needed.

General information only: A Society in Tamil Nadu is registered under the Tamil Nadu Societies Registration Act, 1975 with the Registrar of Societies. Generally it needs at least 7 members, Memorandum and Rules, and office address. A Charitable Trust is created by a Trust Deed on stamp paper, executed and registered at the Sub-Registrar office, with settlor, trustees, objects, and property details. Generally needed: ID / address proofs of members / trustees, photos, office proof, and governing document. This is not advice on a specific registration. Reference: TN Societies Registration Act, 1975 and Indian Trusts Act - general procedure.

General information on what a Legal Opinion means and when people generally seek it.

FAQ Manager — Admin List (20 FAQs) — Edit + Remove + Delete

✅ Ella 20 FAQs-um inga irukku — Edit, Remove / Hide, Delete ellaame pannalam.

1. How do I book a consultation?Consultation • Visible
You can book through the contact form, by calling the office number, or through WhatsApp. Share only general details at first. Do not send original documents or highly confidential papers before consultation.
2. What is the consultation fee?Consultation • Visible
The fee depends on the matter - Civil, Criminal, Family, Property, or Documentation. Contact the office to know the fee before consultation. It will be clearly informed in advance.
3. What documents should I bring for my first consultation?Consultation • Visible
Bring ID proof and all papers relating to your matter - agreements, notices, court papers, property documents, or previous orders if any. Bring originals for verification and keep photocopies / photos ready.
4. How long will my case take?General • Visible
Time depends on type of case, court, documents, and the other side's response. Some matters settle early, some take longer. During consultation you will get a realistic idea of process and timeline.
5. Will my information be kept confidential?General • Visible
Yes. Consultation details and documents will be handled with confidentiality and professional ethics. Do not share case details with others while the matter is pending.
6. Do you handle cases outside Chennai?General • Visible
Matters are mainly handled in Chennai. For matters in other districts or before the High Court, contact the office to check availability and procedure.
7. What is a legal notice? Should I reply to it?Legal Process • Visible
A legal notice is a formal written demand / intimation before filing a case. Do not ignore it. Note the date, keep it safe, and consult with the notice and related papers. Reply, if needed, should be given within the time mentioned.
8. Can my matter be settled without going to court?Legal Process • Visible
In many Civil, Family, Property and Cheque matters, settlement through discussion, mediation or mutual agreement is possible. If settlement is in your interest, it will be explained clearly before any further step.
9. What should I do if I receive a court notice / summons?Legal Process • Visible
Do not ignore it. Note the date mentioned, keep the notice safe, and contact the office immediately with the notice and related papers. Missing a date can affect your case.
10. Does visiting this website create an advocate-client relationship?General • Visible
No. Visiting this website or submitting an enquiry does not by itself create an advocate-client relationship. It starts only after consultation and mutual confirmation.
11. Are case outcomes guaranteed?General • Visible
No legal outcome can be guaranteed. Every matter depends on its own facts, documents, evidence and applicable law. You will be given an honest assessment, not a promise of result.
12. What is a cheque bounce case? What is the time limit?Cheque Cases • Visible
Under Section 138 NI Act, a written demand notice must be sent within 30 days of receiving the bank return memo. If the drawer does not pay within 15 days of receiving the notice, a complaint is generally filed within 1 month after that. Keep cheque copy, return memo, and notice proof safe.
13. What is a DVC case? Who can file it?DVC Cases • Visible
Under the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005, an aggrieved woman can approach a Protection Officer, service provider, police, or the Magistrate directly. Reliefs can include protection, residence, monetary relief, custody and compensation. Each case depends on its facts.
14. What is a writ petition? When is it filed in High Court?High Court • Visible
Under Article 226, a High Court can issue writs - Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto - for enforcement of fundamental rights and for any other purpose. Which writ applies depends on facts. Consult with the full order / papers.
15. What is the difference between an appeal and a writ?High Court • Visible
An appeal asks a higher court to re-examine a lower court order. A writ asks the High Court to direct a public authority or quash an order, under Article 226. The forum and time limit depend on the type of order and law.
16. What papers are needed for a property case?Property • Visible
Generally: sale deed / title deed, patta / chitta, EC, survey / FMB sketch, tax receipts, agreements, and prior orders if any. Keep originals safe and keep copies ready. Which papers are needed depends on the type of property dispute.
17. What papers are needed for a family case?Family • Visible
Generally: ID proof, marriage proof if applicable, birth certificates of children if custody is involved, income details, and any agreements / notices / prior orders. Each family matter depends on its own facts.
18. What is a motor accident claim (MACT)? What is the time limit?Accident Claims • Visible
Compensation for death or injury in a road accident is claimed before the Motor Accident Claims Tribunal under Section 166, Motor Vehicles Act, 1988. Since the 2019 amendment, the claim must generally be filed within 6 months of the accident. Keep FIR, medical papers, bills, and insurance details safe.
19. How is a Society / Trust registered?Registration • Visible
A Society in Tamil Nadu is registered with the Registrar of Societies under the TN Societies Registration Act, 1975, generally with at least 7 members, Memorandum and Rules. A Trust is created by a Trust Deed on stamp paper and registered at the Sub-Registrar office. Documents needed depend on the type of registration.
20. What is a Legal Opinion? How is it different from consultation?Legal Opinion • Visible
A Legal Opinion is a written view on a legal question after studying the documents given. A consultation is a discussion. An opinion is generally given in writing after full papers are seen. Incomplete papers can change the view.
